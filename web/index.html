Two mistakes, almost exactly cancelling.

What you get back

Three numbers. The second two are the product.

Every reconciliation tool reports the first one.

90.9%
Match rate
"Did the money arrive?"
Batches whose total agrees with a bank credit. It nearly always passes — Razorpay pays exactly what its own report says, so the two agree by construction.
What everyone reports
73.0%
Proof rate
"Can I show every deduction was correct?"
Lines that trace end to end with no link missing: order → fee → tax → batch → bank credit. Break one link and the line is not proven, however well the totals agree.
What we report
68.2%
False-match rate
"How many matches fall apart when attacked?"
Of the batches that passed, this share was overturned by an adversarial pass that tries to break its own results. Published on purpose.
What nobody reports
Why you can trust these. The benchmark is built truth-first: the real ledger is generated and frozen, documents are derived from it, then errors are injected into the documents only. Four defect classes were planted with no detector written for them — three were caught anyway, one is reported as missed. Recall on what we designed for is 100%, which proves nothing. Recall on the held-out classes is 75%. That is the number that means something.

How it works

Two files you already have

You do not need to understand reconciliation to use this.

01~1 min

Download two files

Your settlement report and your bank statement for the same month, both as CSV. Anything else you have makes the answer sharper.

Settlements — Razorpay Dashboard → Settlements → Reports
Bank — net-banking portal → statement → export CSV
02~30 sec

Drop them in

Drag both onto the page, type your business name, pick the month. Set your MDR rate if you know it — otherwise 2% is assumed.

Nothing is kept. Statements are read in memory and discarded. Attest stores the close pack, not your files.
03< 1 sec

Read the verdict

A sealed close pack: what tied, what can be proven, what broke under testing, and a ranked list of money you can still claim back.

Then act. Each exception names the counterparty, the evidence that settles it, and the date it expires.

Honest scope

Built in the hackathon, and what comes next

A roadmap presented as a feature is a lie. This is the split.

Shipped and running

  • An AI controller that plans an investigation, step by step
  • 14 deterministic tools that verify — the model never computes money
  • A policy gate: no action available to the AI can certify a close
  • Deterministic match engine with per-line proof chains
  • Adversarial auditor — six falsification hypotheses
  • Agent benchmark: 0% false certification across six scenarios
  • Accuracy scored against held-out ground truth
  • Deadline-aware recovery with counterparty and claim window
  • Tamper-evident close pack, independently verifiable
  • Live Razorpay Settlement Recon API, accounts with row-level security
  • Hosted app, and one command that verifies every claim on this page

Next, in order

  • Razorpay OAuth, so a merchant never pastes a key
  • Webhook ingest, so the close runs daily instead of monthly
  • File the claims, not only draft them
  • A CA-firm console across many client merchants
  • Journal export to Tally and Zoho
  • Learn which claim types actually get paid, and rank by that
  • More defect classes — each still held out before it is scored
Why the benchmark is synthetic, deliberately. Accuracy can only be measured against data whose truth you constructed. On live settlements you can report that something does not reconcile — never whether you were right, because nobody knows the correct answer. So the ingestion path is real and the accuracy benchmark is synthetic, by necessity. A pack built from your own data says so, and shows no recall figure at all.
