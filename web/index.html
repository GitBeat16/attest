What you actually do

You do not need to understand reconciliation to use this. You need two files you already have, and about two minutes.

01~1 min

Download two files

Your settlement report for the month, and your bank statement for the same month. Both as CSV. That is the minimum — anything else you have makes the answer sharper.

Settlements — Razorpay Dashboard → Settlements → Reports → download the recon report for the month

Bank — your net-banking portal → statement for the same month → export as CSV
02~30 sec

Drop them in

Drag both files onto the page. Type your registered business name and pick the month you are closing. If you know your MDR rate, set it — otherwise the standard 2% is assumed.

Nothing is kept. Your statements are read in memory and discarded. Attest stores the close pack it produces, not your raw files.
03under a second

Read three numbers

You get a sealed close pack: what tied, what can be proven, what broke under testing, and a ranked list of money you can still claim back — with the deadline on each claim.

Then act. Each exception names the counterparty, the evidence that would settle it, and the date it expires.
Do not have all the files? Run it anyway. Attest never assumes what it cannot see — a missing source lowers the proof rate and the app tells you exactly what it cost. That is the honest behaviour, and it is shown in full further down.

What you get back

Three numbers, and the second two are the point

Every reconciliation tool reports the first one. Here is what each actually answers, in the order a merchant would ask.

90.9%
Match rate
"Did the money arrive?"
The share of settlement batches whose total agrees with a credit in your bank. It is a real check and it passes almost always — because Razorpay pays exactly what its own report says it will. The two documents agree by construction.
What everyone reports
73.0%
Proof rate
"Can I show why every deduction was correct?"
The share of individual lines that trace end to end with no link missing: the order the customer placed → the fee charged on it → the tax on that fee → the batch it settled in → the credit in your bank. Break any link and the line is not proven, however well the totals agree.
What we report
68.2%
False-match rate
"How many of those matches fall apart when attacked?"
Of the batches that passed, this share was overturned by an adversarial pass that actively tries to break its own results — six hypotheses about how a match could be true for the wrong reason. Published on purpose, because a tool that never reports its own failures is not reporting.
What nobody reports
Why you can trust these numbers. The benchmark data is built truth-first: the real ledger is generated and frozen, the documents are derived from it, then errors are injected into the documents only. Four defect classes were planted with no detector written for them — three were caught anyway by generic integrity checks, and one is reported as missed. Recall on the classes we designed for is 100%, which on its own proves nothing at all. Recall on the held-out classes is 75%. That is the number that means something.

Twenty paise, against ₹8,495.80 of real error inside. Inside any tolerance a finance team would set, so it passes silently and the money leaves. Accounting calls this a compensating error, and a total-level check cannot detect it by construction — which is why a match rate is not evidence of a correct close.

Attest catches it by rebuilding what the refund deduction should have been from your own refund records and the netting rule in your contract — never from the settlement report it is auditing. Use the audited document to check itself and the error cancels out of the very test built to find it.

That gap is not a bug in the measurement — it is the entire argument of this project, and it is why connecting a payment gateway to itself and calling the result reconciled is a category error. The tie tells you money moved. Only evidence tells you the right amount moved.

What you hand your auditor

A close pack that cannot be quietly edited

A report is a file, and a file can be opened and changed. Every close pack carries a SHA-256 digest of the entire rendered document.

4e008d98 18eb491c 63c73b54 5245f684
$ python3 -m attest.seal --verify close-pack.html

INTACT — the whole document hashes to its printed digest. Change one rupee, one date, one letter of the business name, and it reads ALTERED instead.

What it proves, and what it does not

A matching digest proves the pack has not been edited since it was sealed. It does not prove who produced it — anyone holding the code can seal a document. That is true of every self-contained checksum, which is why the honest word is tamper-evident and not tamper-proof.

Authorship comes from the close record: when a close runs, its digest is written to a table with no update policy and no delete policy. A merchant can add a seal; nobody can alter one afterwards — not the merchant, not us. Your auditor asks it one question. Was this exact digest recorded, and when?

Cadence is the product. On the sample month, closing monthly instead of daily lets ₹5,365 across 16 claims expire unfiled — computed from the same findings scored against a close that lands 30 days later, not asserted. Courier disputes die in 14 days. Running daily is not a performance detail; it is the difference between finding money and recovering it.

Honest scope

What exists, and what does not

A roadmap presented as a feature is a lie. This is the split.

Built and working

  • Accounts, with each merchant's closes visible only to them
  • Upload your own exports, or connect a Razorpay test key
  • Live Razorpay Settlement Recon API from the command line
  • Deterministic match engine and per-line proof chains
  • An AI controller that investigates, with tools that prove
  • Adversarial auditor — six falsification hypotheses
  • Accuracy scored against held-out ground truth
  • Deadline-aware recovery with counterparty and claim window
  • Tamper-evident close pack, sealed and independently verifiable
  • One-command local verification of every claim on this page

Roadmap — not built

  • Razorpay OAuth, so merchants never paste a key at all
  • Continuous ingest via webhooks, replacing the batch run
  • A CA-firm view across many client merchants
  • Filing and tracking claims, not only drafting them
  • Journal export to Tally and Zoho
  • A learning loop on which claim types actually get paid
Why the benchmark is synthetic, deliberately. Accuracy can only be measured against data whose truth you constructed. On live settlements you can report that something does not reconcile — never whether you were right, because nobody knows the correct answer. Recall needs an answer key. So the ingestion path is real and the accuracy benchmark is synthetic, by necessity rather than convenience. A close pack produced from your own data says so on its face, and shows no recall figure at all.
